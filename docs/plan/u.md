### The UX bar (applies to every slice U8 to U13; the user's priority)

The goal is the best experience in its class: clear, calm, fast to understand, and alive. Every slice is judged on this, not only on the checklist.

- **Flow first.** Before building a screen, write its flow in the brief: what the owner is trying to do, the fewest steps, what they see after each step, where they go next. One obvious primary action per screen. No dead ends: every empty, loading, error and success state says what happened and what to do next. Keyboard path for every flow (Tab order, Enter, Esc, `Ctrl+K`, `Ctrl+B`).
- **Clarity.** Plain words, short labels, sentence case, no jargon. State is always visible (which teammate, what it is doing, whether a computer is live, whether something waits on the owner). Destructive or outward actions are confirmed once, with the consequence named.
- **Everything gives feedback.** Every press, hover, focus, toggle, send, drop, save and error has a visible response within 100 ms: hover lift or tint, press scale (.97), focus ring, a loading state that appears instantly (skeleton or spinner, never a frozen button), optimistic updates with a quiet undo or retry, success confirmation (a toast, a check, the orb's `done` pose). Nothing changes on screen without a transition.
- **Smooth motion.** Every enter, exit, layout change and state change is animated: shared-element moves (orb into the header, composer docking), list items entering and reordering, sidebar and panel slide with spring physics, page transitions, message streaming, the orb's eyes and poses, suggestion pill hover. Use the `@mawl/ui` motion tokens (springs and durations), 60 fps, transform and opacity only, no layout thrash. `prefers-reduced-motion` and the in-app toggle swap motion for short fades. Motion must explain (where something came from, where it went), never decorate or slow the owner down: interactions never wait on an animation, and nothing animates longer than 400 ms except ambient loops.
- **Libraries are allowed.** Motion (already used) stays the base. A slice may add a maintained external library where it clearly beats hand-rolling (for example `@formkit/auto-animate` for list reorders, `vaul` for drawers, `sonner` toasts already in, `lottie`/`rive` only for a real asset, View Transitions API for page changes). Each addition: check license, maintenance and bundle size, pin the exact version, note it in docs/STACK.md, and keep the main chunk small (lazy-load anything heavy).
- **Responsive everywhere.** 1440, 1100, 760 and 390 px in light and dark, every palette from U9. Touch targets 44 px on coarse pointers, safe areas, sidebar as a drawer under 760 px, composer and panels that never jump when the keyboard opens, no horizontal scroll, content that reflows instead of clipping.
- **Perceived speed.** Instant skeletons, no layout shift, optimistic sends, prefetch the next likely view, and a first paint under one second on the desktop app.
- **How it is verified.** Each UI slice closes with a flow walkthrough in the real running app (not only DOM tests): click through the main flows, check every interaction has feedback and a transition, record a short GIF or screenshots at 4 widths in both themes, and list anything that still feels abrupt. The reviewer is told to judge feel and flow, not only correctness.
